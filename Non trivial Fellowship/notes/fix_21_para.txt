This suggests a more specific design hypothesis than simply maximising affinity. A conventional capture antibody is raised by immunisation, so it targets the same immunodominant surfaces the patient's own antibodies target, and competes with them for the same sites. A designed binder carries no such constraint. If immune-complex masking contributes materially to missed NS1 detection, then aiming at a surface the human response largely ignores could reduce that competition, and this is the one structural advantage available to a designed binder that an antibody-based test cannot copy. It remains a prediction rather than a demonstrated advantage. Acting on it would require an epitope-level map of the dominant human anti-NS1 response, which we did not have when the target window was locked. Our epitope, residues 261 to 305, was chosen on rigidity and DENV1-4 conservation (Section 4.1), and Section 7.3 sets out the mapping work that selecting on immunodominance would require.
