1. IS THE BINDER ITSELF SOUND?  -->  YES. nb_3 is not the problem.
================================================================================

FOLD: canonical. ANARCI assigns chain type H, e-value 3.5e-54, germline
IGHV3-23*04 (the classic VHH framework) at 83% V-identity. Secondary structure
is a proper immunoglobulin beta-sandwich: 43 strand residues (~9 strands) and no
spurious helices. No structural anomalies, no chain breaks, no unusual
disulfide geometry. Fold quality is not a failure mode here.

CDR LOOPS (Chothia, mapped to sequential residue numbering of the 118-mer):
  CDR-H1   26-32    GGDLSQY      (7 res)
  CDR-H2   52-57    SADGKK       (6 res)
  CDR-H3   99-108   RDPRTGIWRY   (10 res)  <- longest, primary paratope
All three CDRs cluster on one contiguous face; the paratope centroid sits 16.8 A
from the framework core centroid, i.e. a normal, solvent-presented binding face.

CDR-H3 IS WELL-STRUCTURED AND CORRECTLY ORIENTED. It is fully coil (no
spurious secondary structure), protrudes 18.6 A from the framework core - the
most protruding element of the molecule - and points outward from the binding
face, exactly as a nanobody's antigen-contact loop should. It is not collapsed
back onto the framework and it is not disordered.

PARATOPE SURFACE / CHARACTER (relative SASA on the monomer, >0.25 = exposed):
  CDR-H1  G26 0.50, G27 0.38, D28 0.44, S30 0.29, Q31 0.46      (L29, Y32 buried)
  CDR-H2  D54 0.49, G55 0.29, K56 0.74, K57 0.55                (S52, A53 partial)
  CDR-H3  R99 0.27, P101 0.39, R102 0.75, T103 0.44, G104 0.39,
          I105 0.48, R107 0.44                                  (W106, Y108 partial)

  *** THE PARATOPE IS STRONGLY CATIONIC. ***
  CDR-H3 alone exposes THREE arginines (R99, R102, R107) = net +3, flanked by a
  hydrophobic tip (I105 / W106 / Y108). CDR-H2 adds two more exposed lysines
  (K56, K57). This is the single most important chemical fact in this report,
  and it is what steers nb_3 to the wrong site (section 3).

VERDICT: the binder is sound. Fold, loop lengths, H3 structure and H3
orientation are all canonical. The defect is not in the binder's structure --
it is in the CHEMISTRY of its paratope and in the TARGET definition.

2. SEED-5 ON-EPITOPE POSE - WHAT IS RIGHT AND WRONG
================================================================================

WHAT IS RIGHT: the pose is on-epitope and reproducible. Contacts land on the
beta-ladder at NS1 269, 281, 299, 303, 304, 305, 306, and hotspot 269 is a
genuine 3.9 A contact. Prior work showed 3 of 6 Boltz-2 seeds reproduce this
binding mode, so it is a stable local solution, not a lucky draw.

WHAT IS WRONG - four things, all quantitative:

(a) THE INTERFACE IS TOO SMALL.
    BSA = 354 A^2 per side (SASA: complex 11628, nb_3 alone 6374, target alone
    5962; total buried 708 A^2). Real antibody/nanobody-antigen interfaces bury
    ~600-900 A^2 per side. 354 A^2 is MARGINAL - roughly half a real interface.
    This is consistent with a weak, transient association, not a binder.

(b) THE WRONG PART OF THE BINDER IS DOING THE WORK.
    Contact attribution by binder region:
        CDR-H2      -> NS1 269                     (the one hotspot contact)
        CDR-H3      -> NS1 281, 305                (periphery only)
        FRAMEWORK   -> NS1 299, 303, 304, 305, 306 (the bulk of the interface)
    The primary antigen-contact loop, CDR-H3, contributes only two peripheral
    epitope contacts and NO hotspot. The interface is carried mainly by
    FRAMEWORK residues. Framework-mediated binding is not designable, is not
    affinity-maturable, and is a hallmark of a pose that is settling for
    whatever contact geometry it can find rather than engaging the target.

(c) ONLY 1 OF 5 HOTSPOTS IS ENGAGED, AND THE REST ARE NOT MARGINALLY MISSED.
    Hotspot min heavy-atom distances from any binder atom:
        269   3.9 A   ENGAGED
        272   7.5 A   ~3 A out  (one CDR extension away)
        274   7.7 A   ~3 A out  (one CDR extension away)
        296  12.2 A   far
        294  19.0 A   very far
    272 and 274 are within reach of a loop change. 294 and 296 are NOT
    (section 4 explains why this is a target-geometry problem, not a loop one).

(d) APPROACH GEOMETRY: the binder does NOT land flat on the ladder.
    Binder long axis vs. beta-ladder surface normal = 29 deg, i.e. nb_3 comes in
    close to head-on/perpendicular rather than lying flat across the sheet.
    A tilted, near-perpendicular approach on a FLAT target (section 4) presents
    only a small contact footprint - which is exactly what the 354 A^2 BSA and
    the single hotspot contact reflect. A flat epitope rewards a binder that
    lies across it; nb_3 is planted on one end of it at an angle.
    Additionally, in the full-length context this pose also touches the TIP
    region (324-328, 346) - the host-mimicry zone the project explicitly avoids.

WHAT WOULD BE NEEDED TO RECRUIT 272 AND 274?
    Both sit 7.5 / 7.7 A away, and both are in the SAME spatial cluster as the
    already-engaged 269 (269-272 = 8.5 A, 272-274 = 7.2 A, 269-274 = 12.9 A CA-CA;
    all three span only 12.9 A, which fits inside one VHH paratope footprint).
    So this specific sub-goal is a LOOP problem and is achievable: extending /
    redesigning CDR-H3 (currently reaching only peripheral 281/305) to sweep
    across the 269-272-274 face, ideally combined with a modest rigid-body tilt
    to lay the paratope flatter on the sheet, would bring all three into contact.
    It does NOT require a different scaffold.
    IT WILL NOT, HOWEVER, REACH 294/296 - see section 4.

3. OFF-EPITOPE POSE - WHY THE N-TERMINUS BEATS THE BETA-LADDER
================================================================================

Recovered seed-3 (ipTM 0.778, pLDDT 0.910) binds the NS1 N-TERMINUS:
contacts at NS1 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15 (plus 28, 29, 190, 214, 324).
This exactly reproduces the historical seed-3 contact set (1-15), confirming the
recovery is faithful. Recovered seed-4 (ipTM 0.713) binds a different off-target
patch (35-40 / 110-122 / 160-168).

HEAD-TO-HEAD, THE DECOY IS A BETTER INTERFACE BY EVERY STRUCTURAL MEASURE:

                            seed-5 (beta-ladder)   seed-3 (N-terminus)
    Boltz-2 ipTM                   0.752                 0.778
    complex pLDDT                  0.893                 0.910
    BSA per side                 354 A^2               849 A^2   (2.4x LARGER)
    interface driven by         FRAMEWORK              CDR-H3    (correct loop)
    NS1-side net charge          +1 (basic)            -1 (acidic)

WHY IT WINS - TWO INDEPENDENT REASONS:

(i) CHARGE COMPLEMENTARITY. This is the decisive factor.
    nb_3's paratope is net +3 (R99/R102/R107 on CDR-H3, plus K56/K57 on H2).
    - The N-terminal patch it actually binds is ACIDIC: D1, E12 among contacts
      1-15, net charge -1. Cationic paratope + anionic patch = attractive,
      charge-matched, and CDR-H3 buries straight into it (H3 contacts NS1
      10-13, 28-29).
    - The beta-ladder contact set is the OPPOSITE: H269, R299, K306 give net
      +1, i.e. BASIC. A +3 paratope approaching a +1 surface is electrostatically
      REPELLED.
    nb_3 is chemically built to prefer the N-terminus. The design is fighting
    its own paratope charge every time it is pushed onto the ladder.

(ii) SHAPE / BURIABILITY. The N-terminal region offers a groove-like,
     strand-edge environment that accommodates a protruding CDR-H3 and buries
     849 A^2. The beta-ladder is flat (section 4) and buries only 354 A^2 at the
     same 4.5 A criterion. More burial per unit of loop = a deeper energy well,
     which is what the confidence metric is reading.

IS THE DECOY AVOIDABLE BY TARGET CROPPING ALONE?  --  NO, NOT RELIABLY.
Cropping the N-terminus removes THAT decoy, but it does not remove the cause.
Evidence that the attraction is a property of nb_3, not of one NS1 site:
  - seed-4 finds a DIFFERENT off-target patch (35-40/110-122/160-168) at high
    ipTM (0.713). Deleting residues 1-15 does not touch it.
  - The already-cropped seed-5 construct (target = 255-310 only) STILL produces
    a framework-carried, 354 A^2, single-hotspot interface - cropping bought
    on-epitope placement but not a good interface.
  - In the full-length mixed pose, nb_3 additionally grabs the TIP region
    (326-328, 344-346) - a third off-target surface, and one with host-mimicry
    / false-positive risk.
A +3 paratope on a target with multiple acidic patches will keep finding them.
Cropping is a necessary hygiene measure; the actual fix is to REBALANCE THE
PARATOPE CHARGE (section 5).

4. THE BETA-LADDER SURFACE - WHAT IT OFFERS A BINDER (AND WHAT IT LACKS)
================================================================================

Measured on properly folded full-length NS1 (9W22 chain A).

WHAT IT LACKS - (a) CONCAVITY:
    Best-fit-plane RMS over the 19 CA atoms within 12 A of the hotspot centroid
    = 3.32 A. The patch is essentially FLAT. There is no pocket, cleft, or
    concavity for a binder to grip. Flat epitopes are the hardest class of de
    novo binder target: there is nothing to key into, so affinity must come
    entirely from broad, shape-matched surface complementarity - which in turn
    demands a large, flat, well-matched paratope lying across the sheet.

WHAT IT LACKS - (b) A DOMINANT CHEMICAL FEATURE:
    Exposed beta-ladder residues (relative SASA > 0.20):
      H261 T264 A265 H269 R272 E274 D278 F279 E281 G282 T284 V286 V287 T288
      E289 D290 G292 N293 R294 P296 T301 A303 S304 G305
    Character breakdown: 9 hydrophobic / 6 polar / 5 acidic / 4 basic.
    This is a BLAND, MIXED surface - a dispersed patchwork with no contiguous
    hydrophobic patch to drive burial and no coherent charge field to give a
    designable electrostatic handle. All five hotspots are solvent-exposed
    (269 relSASA 0.47, 272 0.52, 274 0.36, 294 0.33, 296 0.51), so accessibility
    is not the issue - distinctiveness is.

*** THE CENTRAL FINDING: THE FIVE HOTSPOTS ARE NOT ONE EPITOPE. ***
    Pairwise CA-CA distances (full-length NS1):
        269-272   8.5 A          294-296   7.2 A
        272-274   7.2 A
        269-274  12.9 A
        ---- across the gap ----
        269-296  11.8 A  <- closest cross-cluster pair, WITHIN 15 A reach
        274-296  17.4 A     272-296  17.8 A
        269-294  18.6 A     274-294  21.6 A     272-294  23.6 A
    Single-linkage clustering at 10 A returns TWO clusters:
        CLUSTER A = {269, 272, 274}   span 12.9 A
        CLUSTER B = {294, 296}        span  7.2 A
        cross-cluster pair distances span 11.8 - 23.6 A
    The hotspots ARE co-planar (0.5 A RMS from a best-fit plane) - so this is not
    a curvature problem. They are simply SPREAD TOO FAR APART along a flat sheet.

    IMPORTANT PRECISION POINT: it is NOT true that every cross-cluster pair is
    out of reach. The closest cross-cluster pair, 269-296, is only 11.8 A apart -
    within the ~15 A single-paratope footprint - so that PAIR could in principle
    be engaged together. The impossibility is a property of TRIPLES, not pairs,
    and it is established by exhaustive enumeration rather than by the cluster
    separation alone. All ten 3-hotspot subsets and their spans:
        269+272+274   12.9 A   <-- FITS (the only one)
        269+274+296   17.4 A
        269+272+296   17.8 A
        272+274+296   17.8 A
        269+294+296   18.6 A
        269+274+294   21.6 A
        274+294+296   21.6 A
        269+272+294   23.6 A
        272+274+294   23.6 A
        272+294+296   23.6 A
    EXACTLY ONE of the ten 3-subsets fits within a ~15 A paratope footprint:
    {269, 272, 274}. No 4-subset fits either - all five 4-subset spans:
        269+272+274+296  17.8 A  <-- smallest
        269+274+294+296  21.6 A
        269+272+274+294  23.6 A
        269+272+294+296  23.6 A
        272+274+294+296  23.6 A
    (full 5-hotspot span 23.6 A). Therefore
    the locked gate ">= 3 of 5 hotspots" is satisfiable ONLY by the single
    combination {269,272,274}, and is unsatisfiable by any subset that mixes the
    two clusters. Since the gate as written accepts any 3 of the 5, it reads as
    far more permissive than it actually is - in practice it has exactly one
    solution, and that solution is cluster A. No amount of diffusion, loop
    sampling, or reseeding changes this - the constraint is in the target
    definition, not in the designs.

IS THERE A MORE DESIGNABLE SUB-PATCH?  --  YES: CLUSTER A, {269, 272, 274}.
    - Geometrically coherent: 7.2-12.9 A apart, fits one paratope.
    - Already partly engaged: seed-5 holds 269 at 3.9 A, with 272/274 only
      7.5/7.7 A out - i.e. within reach of a CDR-H3 redesign.
    - Fully conserved across DENV1-4 (verified against the project alignment,
      not assumed from sequence):
          269 H/H/H/H   270 L/L/L/L   271 G/G/G/G
          272 K/K/K/K   273 L/L/L/L   274 E/E/E/E
      The whole contiguous 269-274 stretch is invariant across all four
      serotypes, so a binder confined to cluster A retains full pan-serotype
      coverage. (Note: the campaign target file carries R at 272 whereas all
      four DENV reference sequences have K - a conservative K/R substitution at
      a conserved basic position, but worth correcting in the target input.)
    - Chemically it offers a real handle that the full patch does not: H269 +
      K272 (basic) adjacent to E274 (acidic) is a mixed-charge triad a designed
      paratope can complement directionally - provided the paratope is not
      uniformly +3 (section 3).
    Cluster B {294, 296} should be dropped from the objective. R294/P296 are
    conserved, but they are unreachable in the same binding event and 296 sits
    near the disordered end of the crop.

5. DIAGNOSIS AND RECOMMENDATION
================================================================================

THE CORE ISSUE, IN PLAIN LANGUAGE (the answer to the mentor's question):

  There are TWO problems, and neither one is a bad binder backbone.

  PROBLEM 1 - THE TARGET DEFINITION IS ALL BUT UNSATISFIABLE (dominant).
  The five "hotspots" are not one binding site. They form two clusters -
  {269,272,274} and {294,296} - with cross-cluster pair distances of 11.8-23.6 A
  on a flat beta-sheet. One nanobody paratope spans ~15 A. Enumerating all ten
  possible 3-hotspot combinations, EXACTLY ONE fits inside that footprint:
  {269,272,274} (span 12.9 A). No 4-subset fits at all (smallest 4-subset span
  17.8 A, for {269,272,274,296}). So the locked gate
  (">= 3 of 5 hotspots") looks permissive but has precisely one solution, and
  every mixed-cluster combination it nominally allows is geometrically
  forbidden. (One nuance, for accuracy: the closest cross-cluster PAIR, 269-296
  at 11.8 A, IS individually reachable - the impossibility applies to triples,
  not to every pair.) Designs have effectively been failing a test with one
  hidden right answer, and the campaign has been reading that as design failure. On top of that, the patch is
  flat (3.3 A plane-RMS - no pocket) and chemically bland (9 hydrophobic /
  6 polar / 5 acidic / 4 basic), which makes it a genuinely hard de novo target
  even after the gate is fixed.

  PROBLEM 2 - THE PARATOPE IS CHARGED FOR THE WRONG SURFACE.
  nb_3's CDR-H3 presents three arginines (R99/R102/R107, net +3, plus K56/K57 on
  CDR-H2). The beta-ladder contact surface is itself basic (H269/R299/K306, net
  +1) - so the design is electrostatically repelled from its own target. The NS1
  N-terminus, by contrast, is acidic (D1/E12, net -1) and charge-matched, which
  is why CDR-H3 buries into it and produces a 849 A^2 interface at ipTM 0.778
  while the on-target pose manages only 354 A^2 at ipTM 0.752. This is the
  mechanism behind the ipTM/epitope-fidelity inversion the project has been
  seeing: the confidence metric is not broken, it is correctly reporting that
  the off-epitope interface is genuinely better. The binder prefers the wrong
  site because it was built to.

  Symptom that follows from both: on target, the interface is carried by
  FRAMEWORK residues (299, 303-306) with CDR-H3 reduced to two peripheral
  contacts. That is the structural signature of a binder that cannot productively
  engage the surface it has been aimed at.

SO WHICH KIND OF FIX IS IT? -- It is primarily a TARGET-SURFACE / HOTSPOT-
DEFINITION issue, secondarily a PARATOPE-CHEMISTRY issue, and only thirdly a
CDR-loop issue. Partial diffusion of CDR loops alone will NOT clear the gate:
it would optimise a charge-mismatched interface against an unsatisfiable
objective. Ranked recommendations:

  1. REDEFINE THE OBJECTIVE TO CLUSTER A ONLY -- {269, 272, 274}. [do first,
     costs nothing]
     Drop 294/296 from the hotspot set and from the gate. Replace ">= 3 of 5
     hotspots" with ">= 3 of 3 in cluster A" (or >= 2 of 3 plus a BSA floor).
     Justification is structural and now documented: 17-24 A separation, single
     paratope span ~15 A. Cluster A is fully DENV1-4 conserved (269 H, 272 K,
     274 E, with 270/271/273 also invariant), so pan-serotype coverage is
     retained - verified against the alignment, not assumed.
     ** RETRACTED: an earlier version of this document proposed a BSA floor of
     >= 600 A^2 per side. Section 6 shows that is physically unachievable on
     beta-ladder-only contacts (ceiling ~370 A^2) and that imposing it would
     select for designs burying the forbidden tip. Use instead: epitope-only
     burial >= 300 A^2 AND forbidden-tip burial <= 40 A^2, reported separately. **
     Also fix the 272 R->K discrepancy in the target input.

  2. REBALANCE THE PARATOPE CHARGE. [the real design fix]
     Reduce the +3 on CDR-H3. Retain ONE arginine positioned to pair with E274,
     and mutate the other two toward polar/small (S/T/N/G) or, where a
     directional pair is wanted, acidic residues that can engage H269/K272.
     Target roughly net 0 to +1 across the CDRs. This attacks the N-terminus
     decoy at its cause rather than hiding it, and it should reduce the
     off-target attraction to the tip region (326-328, 344-346) as well.

  3. THEN redesign CDR-H3 geometry to sweep cluster A. [now worth doing]
     H3 is currently well-structured and correctly oriented but reaches only
     peripheral 281/305. With 272/274 just 7.5/7.7 A out and in the same cluster
     as the engaged 269, extending/reshaping H3 - plus a modest rigid-body tilt
     to lay the paratope flatter than the current 29 deg approach and shift work
     off the framework - is a well-posed loop problem. Seed-5 remains the
     correct scaffold to seed from. Do this AFTER 1 and 2, or it will optimise
     the wrong objective again.

  4. KEEP the target crop, but do not rely on it. Crop out the N-terminus (1-25)
     and the tip (311-352) during design as hygiene. Cropping alone is
     insufficient: seed-4 finds a THIRD off-target patch (35-40/110-122/160-168)
     that cropping 1-15 does not touch, and the already-cropped seed-5 construct
     still yielded a marginal framework-carried interface.

  5. IF, after 1-3, BSA still cannot be pushed past ~600 A^2 per side on
     cluster A, the honest conclusion is that this flat, bland patch is not a
     tractable single-domain de novo target. At that point the options are a
     larger/flatter scaffold presenting more paratope area (a minibinder or
     three-helix design rather than a VHH), or a documented negative result on
     Direction B. Both are legitimate outputs for the benchmark, and the
     structural evidence assembled here is exactly what makes such a negative
     result publishable rather than an unexplained failure.

6. ADDENDUM - THE BURIAL CEILING, AND WHY THE >=600 A^2 FLOOR WAS WRONG
================================================================================
(Added after a follow-up check. This SUPERSEDES recommendation 1's BSA floor and
materially changes the outlook. It arose from removing a confound in section 3:
the 354-vs-849 A^2 comparison mixed a CROPPED target with FULL-LENGTH ones.)

CONTROLLING FOR THE CROP. Per-region decomposition of target-side burial:

  pose                target form   total   beta-ladder   tip 311-352   N-term
  seed5 (on-epitope)  CROPPED       375     356 (95%)     0             0
  noMSA (mixed)       FULL 1-346    859     373 (44%)     359 (42%)     0
  seed3 (off-epitope) FULL 1-346    868       9 ( 1%)      28 ( 3%)     546 (63%)

  The crop was NOT the confound: beta-ladder burial is 356 A^2 cropped vs
  373 A^2 full-length - essentially identical. So the section-3 conclusion
  stands. But the decomposition reveals something worse:

  *** THE noMSA POSE ONLY REACHED 859 A^2 BY BURYING THE FORBIDDEN TIP. ***
  42% of that "good" interface is the host-mimicry region 311-352 the project
  explicitly avoids. Its beta-ladder contribution is 373 A^2 - the same ceiling
  as seed-5. Large BSA on this target is achievable only by cheating.

THE CEILING IS STRUCTURAL, NOT A DESIGN FAILURE.
  Total exposed SASA of the whole beta-ladder 261-305 on free NS1: 2357 A^2.
  But within a 12 A radius of the cluster-A centroid, the beta-ladder-only
  accessible pool is just 387 A^2. At a realistic 50-60% occlusion, a
  cluster-A-focused paratope can bury only ~194-232 A^2 of epitope
  (0.50 x 387 = 194; 0.60 x 387 = 232).
  Observed on-epitope burial (356-373 A^2) is therefore already AT OR ABOVE
  what this patch offers a focused binder - nb_3 is not underperforming the
  surface; it has essentially saturated it.

  => THE >= 600 A^2 PER SIDE FLOOR I PROPOSED IN RECOMMENDATION 1 IS
     PHYSICALLY UNACHIEVABLE ON BETA-LADDER-ONLY CONTACTS. Imposing it would
     have selected precisely for designs that bury the forbidden tip - i.e. it
     would have actively rewarded the false-positive risk the project set out
     to avoid. Retract it. Replace with:
        - epitope-only burial >= 300 A^2 (achievable; seed-5 is at 356)
        - AND forbidden-tip burial <= 40 A^2 (a HARD ceiling, new)
     Report the two numbers separately and never sum them into one BSA.

WHY NO SUB-PATCH ESCAPES THE TIP. Min heavy-atom distance, hotspot to tip:
        269 -> tip 330 at 3.9 A        294 -> tip 334 at 2.4 A
        272 -> tip 325 at 4.3 A        296 -> tip 336 at 2.7 A
        274 -> tip 324 at 3.0 A
  These are direct van der Waals contacts. The epitope and the avoid-window are
  interdigitated strands of the SAME beta-sheet, not neighbouring surfaces. A
  15 A paratope footprint centred on cluster A spans 18 beta-ladder residues AND
  12 forbidden tip residues (321-332). Cluster B is even more tip-entangled than
  cluster A, so switching sub-patch does not help.
  CONSEQUENCE: "target the beta-ladder but avoid the tip" is not two independent
  constraints that a better design can satisfy simultaneously - they are in
  direct geometric tension. Any binder large enough to bury a real interface
  here will contact 311-352. This is the deepest reason Direction B has resisted
  five rounds of design effort, and it was invisible while BSA was being read as
  a single lumped number.

  Note: the project's DENV1-4 conservation artifact covers only 261-305, so
  whether the avoid-window can be safely NARROWED (e.g. is all of 311-352
  really host-mimicking, or only a sub-stretch?) cannot be answered from
  existing artifacts. That question is now on the critical path - see step 1
  below - because if the true mimicry motif is narrow and excludes 321-332,
  Direction B reopens.

WHAT THIS SESSION DOES NOT SHOW
-------------------------------
No wet-lab validation is implied; all numbers are from predicted structures.
Boltz-2 confidence is trusted here only for binder-binding ranking (as the
project's harness validation established), and this analysis deliberately relies
on geometry and chemistry (BSA, distances, planarity, charge) rather than on
confidence scores - the ipTM inversion is precisely why. BSA values are
single-structure FreeSASA numbers without ensemble averaging or relaxation, so
treat +/- ~50 A^2 as noise; the seed-5 vs seed-3 gap (354 vs 849) is far larger
than that. The two-cluster hotspot geometry is a property of the NS1 crystal
structure (9W22) and is independent of any prediction.
